The Permanent Collection

Messes found in nearly every building we walk through. None of them are anyone's fault. They're what happens when smart people solve real problems under pressure. Recognize any?

Untitled (The Spreadsheet Nobody Owns)

2019–present · Excel, email attachments, one person's memory

Began as a quick fix. Now has 43 tabs, three versions labeled FINAL, and powers the board report. Its creator left in 2022. Nobody dares sort column F.

The fixFind who actually uses it, give it an owner, retire the tabs nobody reads. Then decide whether it needs to become something else.

Seventeen Tools, One Workflow

2021 · SaaS subscriptions, abandoned integrations, annual auto-renewals

Each tool was bought to solve a real problem. Together they create four new ones. Data is copied by hand between three of them, every day, by someone who should be doing something else.

The fixInventory what you pay for and what gets used. Consolidate, cancel, or connect, with the switching cost of each written down first.

Study in Handoffs

Ongoing · Chat threads, shared inboxes, hope

A customer is sold on Tuesday and discovered by operations on Friday. Everyone did their job. The work fell through the gap between the jobs.

The fixMap the handoff as it really happens, give each step one owner, and measure the time between steps, not just the steps themselves.

The Pilot That Never Landed

2024 · Large language model, slide deck, press release

Stunning in the demo. Quietly shelved in production, where the data was messier, the edge cases were many, and nobody owned what happened when it was wrong.

The fixTest on your data, not the demo set. Clean what it depends on first. Decide who owns the errors before launch, not after.

The Work

Five services, each with a clear scope and a clear way to tell whether it worked. We price by the job wherever we can, so you know the cost before we start.

01

The Walkthrough

We walk every room before we touch anything.

A diagnostic of your operations, tools, and handoffs. We start by talking to the people closest to the work. You get a Condition Report covering what's broken, roughly what it costs, what to fix first, and what to leave alone.

You keepThe report and the baseline, whether or not you hire us again. It'll say when you don't need us.

02

The Deep Clean

Untangle the specific mess.

An operational cleanup scoped from the Walkthrough. We redesign the process with the people who run it, fix or remove the tools involved, document the new way, and train the owner.

Measured byTwo to four metrics agreed in writing before we start, reported against the baseline, including any that didn't move.

03

The Supply Closet

What you pay for, what you use, what's held together with tape.

A tools and technology inventory. We look at licenses, usage, owners, overlaps, and single points of failure, then recommend what to keep, consolidate, replace, or cancel, with the switching cost of each.

Measured byAnnual spend, tool count, undocumented integrations, and time to onboard a new hire.

04

The AI Reality Check

An honest inspection of what's ready and what's a demo.

We test a specific AI use case against your data, your edge cases, and your volume, and check whether your process and data are ready to support it.

Measured byAccuracy on your real cases, review time, cost per transaction, and error rate.

05

The Night Shift

Keeping it clean.

A light monthly retainer. We review the metrics we set up together, fix small things before they become big ones, and give you a second opinion before you buy the next tool.

TermsCapped hours. Cancel any month.

On AI

We're not anti-AI. We're anti-pretending.

Some AI tools are genuinely useful today, and we'll help you use them well. Many others are a demo, a press release, and a pilot that never reaches production. They fail because the data, the process, and the ownership underneath weren't ready to hold them up.

Nobody gets excited about cleaning the floor before the new machine arrives. It's still the part that decides whether the machine works.

It's a great new mop. Someone still has to know where the floor is.

Every use case gets one of three verdicts

Ready

It works on your data today. Here's the rollout plan and what to watch.

Not yet

Here's what has to be true first (usually cleanup work) and roughly what it costs.

Not this

It can't reliably do the job. Here's what would work instead. Sometimes that's a better spreadsheet.
