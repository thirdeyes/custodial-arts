# The Custodial Arts Manifesto

A janitor who's been called "an expert in the custodial arts" is the butt of a joke. The joke only works because it's true.

The custodian is the one person who has a key to every room. They know which pipe leaks and which door sticks. They know which department leaves a mess every Friday and which one quietly cleans up after everyone else. They see the building as it really is, not as it looks in the brochure. Almost nobody asks for their opinion.

In *The Breakfast Club*, a group of teenagers and an assistant principal spend a Saturday assuming they understand how their school works. The person who actually does is the janitor. He sees everyone for who they are, and nobody ever gives him the floor.

**Custodial Arts is a consultancy for the work that keeps the building standing.**

---

## What we believe

**The essential work is the unglamorous work.**
Every business runs on things nobody puts in a keynote: the handoff between sales and ops, the report someone rebuilds by hand every Monday, the integration that breaks whenever a vendor changes a field name. Most businesses win or lose on this stuff. We think it deserves expert attention, so we give it the fancy name.

**Messes are welcome here.**
We don't flinch at the spreadsheet with 40 tabs or the tool stack nobody can explain. We'd much rather hear "it's bad" than "it's fine." Bring us the mess, we'll bring the elbow grease.

**Measure before you mop.**
We don't start cleaning until we know what "dirty" looks like in numbers. Every engagement starts with a baseline, and every result is reported against it, including the parts that didn't move.

**Say what it is.**
A 12% improvement is a 12% improvement. We won't call it "transformational." If something is going to take six months, we'll say six months. If you don't need us, we'll say that too.

**Your building, your rules.**
We don't install a methodology or make you learn our vocabulary. We work inside how your business already operates, fix what's broken, and leave the rest alone.

**Leave it cleaner than you found it, and leave you able to keep it that way.**
The goal is that you stop needing us. We document what we did, hand over the keys, and teach the people who'll still be there after we leave.

**The frontline knows where the mess is.**
The people closest to the work usually know exactly what's broken. They just haven't been asked, or haven't been listened to. We ask them first.

## On AI

We're not anti-AI. We're anti-pretending.

Some AI tools are genuinely useful today, and we'll help you use them. Many more are a demo, a press release, and a pilot that never makes it to production. The promise often lands on a business whose data, processes, and ownership aren't ready to support it. Nobody gets excited about cleaning the floor before the new machine arrives, but it's the part that decides whether the machine works.

We'll tell you which is which. We'll show our work.

> It's a great new mop. Someone still has to know where the floor is.
